# Fire making and firewood

! Freshly cut wood is wet. Most UK hardwoods need one to two years of seasoning before they burn well. Trees felled during an emergency will not keep you warm for months. Burn your dry store first, and treat felling as preparation for next winter.

## What burns now
- Your seasoned log store, kept off the ground, under cover, with the sides open to the air.
- Standing dead wood (not fallen wood lying on wet ground). It snaps cleanly and feels light.
- Ash is the best of the green woods if you have no choice. It still burns better seasoned.
- Pallets and untreated scrap timber. Avoid painted, varnished or treated wood: the smoke is toxic.
- Cardboard and paper: rolled tight, or soaked, pressed into bricks and dried.

## Three stages of a fire
1. Tinder - catches a spark: cotton wool smeared with petroleum jelly, birch bark, dry grass, cardboard fluff, a firelighter.
2. Kindling - pencil-thin up to thumb-thick dry sticks. Gather twice what you think you need.
3. Fuel - wrist-thick and larger, added once the kindling is burning strongly.

## Lighting it
1. Clear the ground. Make a ring of stones or use a fire pit, well away from fences, sheds, the van and overhanging branches.
2. Put the tinder in the middle and lean kindling over it like a small tent, leaving a gap on the windward side.
3. Light the tinder from the windward side. Feed in smaller kindling first, then larger.
4. Add fuel gradually. Too much too soon smothers it.
5. Keep a bucket of water or sand beside it. Never leave it unattended. Drown it and stir it out completely when you finish.

## Keeping ignition dry
Store matches in a sealed tub with the striker strip. Keep at least two other methods: lighters and a fire steel. Keep tinder in zip bags.

## Cutting and splitting safely
- Wear boots, gloves and eye protection. Work only when rested and in good light.
- Split on a chopping block, so a miss hits the block and not the ground or your shin.
- Clear the full swing arc and keep other people at least two axe lengths away.
- Saw to length first, then split. Small split pieces dry faster.
- An injury is far more serious when help may not come. Stop when you are tired.

## Seasoning
- Split, then stack bark-up in single rows, off the ground on pallets.
- Cover the top but leave the sides open to the wind.
- Wood is ready when it is lighter, the ends are cracked, and two pieces knocked together ring rather than thud.

## Neighbours and smoke
Burn dry wood only. Wet wood gives thick smoke, little heat and annoyed neighbours.
