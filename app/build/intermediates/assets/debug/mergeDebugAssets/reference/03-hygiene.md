# Hygiene with little water

Stomach bugs are the biggest health risk when services fail. Most come from hands and from food left out.

## Handwashing stations
- Put one by the toilet and one by the kitchen: a water container with a tap (or a hanging bottle tipped with a foot loop), soap on a string, and a towel.
- Wash after the toilet, before preparing food, before eating, and after handling waste or canal water.
- Hand sanitiser is a backup. It does not work well on dirty hands, and it does not kill every bug.

## Personal washing on about 2 litres
1. Warm 2 L of water (use heating water left over from boil-in-bag meals).
2. Washcloth: face, armpits, groin and feet daily. A full wash every few days.
3. Brush your teeth with a cup of treated water.
4. Wet wipes cover in-between days. Bag them - don't put them on the compost heap.

## Washing up: two-bowl method
1. Scrape food waste into the bin first.
2. Bowl 1: hot soapy water. Bowl 2: hot rinse water.
3. Air-dry on a rack. Damp tea towels spread germs.

## Food safety without a fridge
- Open tins only as you need them, and eat the contents the same day.
- Keep cooked food covered, and eat it within a couple of hours unless it is cold outside.
- Reheat until piping hot all the way through.
- When in doubt, throw it out. Being ill wastes far more water and energy than the food was worth.

## Rubbish
- Rinse tins and packets so they don't attract rats and flies.
- Keep bin bags in a lidded bin away from the house.
- Paper and cardboard become fire-lighting material. Do not burn plastics: the smoke is toxic.

## Laundry
Prioritise socks and underwear. Hand-wash in a bucket with a plunger or by treading, then wring out and dry under cover. Dry clothes matter more than clean ones in cold weather.
