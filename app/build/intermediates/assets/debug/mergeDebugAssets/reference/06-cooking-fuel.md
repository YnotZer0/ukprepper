# Cooking on limited propane

You have two 6 kg propane bottles. Propane still vaporises in sub-zero weather, which butane does not, so it is the right gas for a UK winter.

## Know your real usage
- Weigh a bottle (bathroom scales) before and after a normal day. Enter the result as "Gas g/day" in Energy & fuel.
- Rough guide only: one burner on full uses something like 100-150 g an hour, so a 6 kg bottle gives very roughly 40-60 burner-hours. Your measurement beats this guess.

## Saving gas
- Always use a lid. It saves a large share of the energy.
- Use the smallest pan that does the job, and a flame that stays under the pan.
- Boil once, then store hot water in a vacuum flask for drinks and washing.
- Cook one-pot meals. Soak pasta or rice in cold water first to shorten cooking.
- Turn off at the bottle when you are not cooking.

## Boil-in-bag ration packs
1. Put the pouch in a pan of water. Bring it to a simmer and heat for the time printed on the pack, usually several minutes.
2. Heat two at once in the same water when two people are eating.
3. Keep the heating water. It is clean enough for washing up, a wash, or a hot-water bottle. Do not drink it untreated if a pouch has split.
4. Ration meals can be eaten cold if gas runs short. They are already cooked.

## Backup heat
Outdoors, a wood fire or small wood-burning stove can boil water and cook. Keep the propane for when the weather or the hour rules out a fire.

## Rotation
The meal planner uses the oldest best-before first. The ration packs dated July 2027 should be eaten or replaced before then. Build them into normal meals over the coming months so none go to waste.
