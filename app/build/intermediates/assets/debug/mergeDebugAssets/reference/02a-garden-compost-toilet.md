# Garden compost toilet (twin pit in the raised area)

The existing hole on the raised area, about 6 ft x 4 ft (1.8 m x 1.2 m), becomes two pits side by side under one lean-to roof against the wall. You use one pit while the other rests. Waste drops straight in, so there are no buckets to carry. Urine goes to a separate tank that overflows into a wood-chip trench (see "Urine: keep it separate").

The sunken area is NOT used. It holds water after several days of heavy rain, and a toilet must stay dry.

## Why this spot works
- The hole drains well, even after rain. Pits must never stand in water.
- The wall gives you the back of the shelter and somewhere to fix the vent pipes.
- The canal is about a quarter of a mile away and the river about half a mile. At that distance a household compost toilet is not a pollution risk to either. The rules that still apply are below.

## Siting rules that still apply
- At least 10 m from any well, pond, ditch or field drain, and not uphill of a water butt you drink from.
- Nut trees: if you gather nuts from the ground, keep compost and urine out of the area where they fall. Nuts picked off contaminated ground are the realistic way germs could get back into your food. Net or rake that ground before harvest.
- Light the route from the house. You will use it at night and in the rain.

## 1. Prepare the hole
1. Empty out the branches and grass cuttings. Chop the branches into short lengths and keep them. Spread the grass cuttings to dry: dried, they become cover material.
2. Measure the depth. Aim for about 1 m of usable depth. If the hole is deeper, backfill the bottom with the chopped branches. If it is shallower, build the collar (step 3) a course or two higher.
3. Check the drainage one more time after a spell of heavy rain. If water ever stands in the bottom for more than a day, raise the floor with more coarse branches and gravel before going on.

## 2. Split it into two pits
1. Build a divider wall across the middle with concrete blocks, from the bottom of the hole up to the top of the collar. That gives two pits of about 0.9 m x 1.2 m each.
2. Leave the bottom of each pit as bare soil, so it keeps draining.
3. Put 25 cm of the chopped branches in the bottom of each pit for air and drainage, then 10 cm of straw or wood chip on top.

## 3. Collar, platform and seats
1. Collar: lay two courses of blocks round the rim of the hole (about 20-30 cm high). This stops rain running in, and it carries the platform.
2. Bank soil against the outside of the collar so it slopes away on every side.
3. Platform: lay timber bearers across the collar and divider. Deck over them in lift-out panels of exterior ply - one panel per pit, big enough to dig through. Cut a seat hole in each panel.
4. Seats: fit a urine-diverting seat insert, or a home-made front funnel, with a hinged lid, to each hole. Pit B's seat gets a board screwed over it until it is needed.
5. Mesh: staple fine wire mesh under the panel edges, so flies and rats can't get in round them.

## 4. Vents and shelter
1. Vents: run a 110 mm soil pipe from under the platform of each pit up past the roof, at least 50 cm above it. Bracket it to the wall, paint it black and cap it with fly mesh. The sun warms the pipe, which draws air down through the seat and out at the top, so smells leave above head height.
2. Roof: a lean-to from the wall, sloping away from the wall and the entrance, with the gutter discharging well away from the pits. Water from it can fill a hand-washing butt (not for drinking).
3. Sides: screening that is open along the top edge for airflow, and a door or heavy curtain at the front.
4. Inside: a hand-wash station (water container with a tap, soap, towel), a lidded bin of dry cover material with a scoop, toilet roll in a lidded box, and a hook for a torch.

## 5. Every visit
1. Use the seat. Urine goes into the front funnel - men should sit down too.
2. Throw in one or two scoops of cover material (sawdust, wood shavings, dried grass, dry leaves, shredded cardboard) so nothing is visible.
3. Close the lid. A closed lid lets the vent pipe draw.
4. Wash your hands with soap and water.

Only human waste, toilet paper and cover material go in the pit. Wipes, sanitary products, plastics and packaging go in the rubbish bin.

## 6. Weekly
- With gloves on, lift the panel edge and level the peak of the pile with a rake kept only for this job, so the pit fills evenly.
- If the pile looks wet or smells, add more dry cover material, and check that the diverter isn't leaking into the pit.
- Check the fly mesh, the soil bank round the collar, and the vent caps.

## 7. Switching and emptying
1. When pit A is about three-quarters full, add a thick final layer of cover material.
2. Screw a board over A's seat and open B.
3. Leave A completely alone for at least 12 months. The Centre for Alternative Technology advises at least a year in the UK climate, because home heaps rarely get hot enough to kill germs quickly.
4. When B is filling up and A has rested at least a year, lift A's panel and dig it out from above. Wear gloves, a dust mask and old clothes. The contents should be dark and crumbly with no recognisable waste. If they aren't, put the panel back and wait longer.
5. Spread the compost around ornamental trees and shrubs - not under the nut trees where nuts are gathered, and never on vegetables. Wash the tools and yourself afterwards.
6. Put a fresh layer of branches and straw in A. It becomes the next pit to fill.

For one or two people a pit this size will probably take many months to fill (an estimate - track yours). During a 90-day emergency you will not need to switch.

## Troubleshooting
- Smell in the shelter: a lid left open, a blocked vent, or a wet pile. Add cover material, and check the diverter and urine pipe for leaks.
- Flies: something is uncovered. Cover thickly, check the mesh, keep the lid shut.
- Water in a pit: a roof or gutter leak, or the soil bank has slumped. Fix the cause, then add plenty of dry straw.
- Diarrhoea in the household: be extra strict with handwashing, wipe the seat after every use, and use extra cover material.

! Never use bleach where it can meet urine - the mix gives off toxic chloramine gas. Clean seats with hot soapy water, then vinegar.

## If the crisis comes before it's built
Use the garage toilet (next pages). Store the full solids bags in a lidded bin outdoors, and add them to pit A once it is ready.
