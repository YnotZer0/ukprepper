# Water: sources, treatment and storage

Water runs out before food does. Ration washing water, never drinking water. Plan on at least 3 litres per person per day to drink; about 10 litres per person per day covers basic cooking and hygiene.

## Sources, best first
1. Stored bottled water.
2. Rainwater from the roof into a clean, lidded water butt. Let the first few minutes of a storm run to waste - it carries dust and bird droppings off the roof.
3. River water: flowing, clear, collected upstream of any outfall pipe, road bridge or farm drain.
4. Canal water: last resort. Still water that boats move through can carry diesel, oil and sewage, as well as Weil's disease.

! Never use floodwater, or water with an oily sheen, a chemical smell, foam, dead fish or blue-green algae. No home treatment makes these safe.

! After heavy rain, rivers can carry sewage overflow. Wait a day or two if you can, and collect upstream.

## Treatment routine - every time, in this order
1. Settle: let it stand in a bucket for 30 minutes so silt sinks.
2. Strain: pour through a clean cloth, tea towel or coffee filter into the DIRTY container.
3. Filter: run it through the Sawyer Mini into the CLEAN container.
4. Disinfect: add purification tablets as the packet says, and wait the full contact time - or bring it to a rolling boil for one minute.
5. Store: in clean, lidded containers marked DRINKING, out of sunlight. Use within a few days.

The Sawyer removes bacteria (including the one that causes Weil's disease) and protozoa such as cryptosporidium and giardia. It does NOT remove viruses, fuel, pesticides or other chemicals. That is why disinfection comes after filtering, and why the choice of source matters so much.

An activated-carbon stage after the Sawyer improves taste and reduces some chemicals. It does not make badly polluted water safe.

## Looking after the Sawyer Mini
- Keep dirty and clean sides apart. Never let dirty water drip onto the clean outlet.
- Backflush with clean water using the syringe whenever the flow slows.
- Do not let a wet filter freeze. Ice can crack the fibres without any visible sign, and a cracked filter lets germs through. In winter keep it in a pocket by day and inside your sleeping bag at night. If it may have frozen, retire it and use the spare.
- Rotate the two filters so you know both work.

## Weil's disease (leptospirosis) when collecting
- Cover cuts and grazes with waterproof plasters. Wear gloves.
- Do not splash canal or river water on your face. Wash and dry your hands after collecting.
- If you get flu-like symptoms within about two weeks of contact with the water, see a doctor and say you have been in contact with canal or river water.

## The van's 70 L tank
Use it for washing, washing up and boiling. Treat it as non-drinking unless it is boiled or tablet-treated. Keep the filler cap clean and closed.

## Rainwater collection
- Use a lid and fine mesh to keep out leaves, insects and mosquitoes.
- Clean the butt before relying on it.
- Each square metre of roof yields about 1 litre per millimetre of rain (before losses). A 20 m² shed or porch roof in 5 mm of rain gives around 100 L.
