# Keeping warm without mains power

## Heat one room, not the house
- Choose the smallest room with the fewest outside walls, ideally one that gets some sun.
- Close every other door. Block draughts under doors with rolled towels.
- Hang blankets over windows at night and open curtains in sunshine.
- Put rugs or cardboard on the floor.

## Layers
- Base layer: wool or synthetic, never cotton next to the skin, because damp cotton chills you.
- Mid layer: fleece or wool jumper. Outer layer: windproof.
- Hat, gloves and thick socks. Keep a dry pair of socks for bed only.
- Loosen layers before you sweat while working; damp clothing loses heat fast.

## Sleeping warm
- Insulation underneath matters more than on top: put a camping mat, folded blankets or cardboard between you and the floor.
- Put the sleeping bag inside a duvet, or lay duvets over it.
- Make a hot-water bottle with boiled water (a sealed metal bottle wrapped in a sock works).
- Eat something before bed, and empty your bladder.
- Two people sharing a bed stay much warmer.

## Food and drink as fuel
Eat regularly and drink warm drinks. Your body burns more energy in the cold, so don't cut calories in winter. Avoid alcohol: it makes you feel warm while you lose heat faster.

## Hypothermia - know the signs
- Early: shivering, cold pale skin, clumsy hands.
- Getting serious: confusion, slurred speech, drowsiness, shivering that stops.
- What to do:
  1. Get the person out of wind and wet, and replace wet clothes with dry.
  2. Insulate them from the ground, wrap them including the head, and share body heat.
  3. Give warm sweet drinks only if they are fully alert.
  4. Rewarm gradually. Never use hot baths or direct heat.
- Call 999 or 112 if the person is confused or drowsy, or if shivering has stopped.

## In the van
A van loses heat quickly. Use thermal screen covers, close the cab curtain, and sleep in the warmest layers.
